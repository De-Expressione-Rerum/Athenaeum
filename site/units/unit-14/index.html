Northern Renaissance and Reformation

1 / 1

Bruegel often makes the ostensible subject easy to overlook: Icarus is almost lost in the landscape, while Christ is hard to find in The Procession to Calvary. Hogenberg, by contrast, makes the destruction of religious images itself the subject.

Holbein belongs here for more than The Body of the Dead Christ. His portrait of Erasmus brings Northern humanism into the same field as religious controversy and sacred imagery: the Unit can hold both the physical force of the dead Christ and the intellectual world in which reform was argued.

Featured Poems and Texts

Auden and Williams return to the course’s opening problem of ekphrasis: compare what each poem selects from Bruegel and what each leaves out.

Musée des Beaux Arts

The first stanza makes the general argument; the second turns to Bruegel and Icarus.

Musée des Beaux Arts

W. H. Auden

Written December 1938.

About suffering they were never wrong,
The Old Masters: how well they understood
Its human position; how it takes place
While someone else is eating or opening a window or just walking dully along
How, when the aged are reverently, passionately waiting
For the miraculous birth, there always must be
Children who did not specially want it to happen, skating
On a pond at the edge of the wood:
They never forgot
That even the dreadful martyrdom must run its course
Anyhow in a corner, some untidy spot
Where the dogs go on with their doggy life and the torturer’s horse
Scratches its innocent behind on a tree.

In Brueghel’s Icarus, for instance: how everything turns away
Quite leisurely from the disaster; the ploughman may
Have heard the splash, the forsaken cry,
But for him it was not an important failure; the sun shone
As it had to on the white legs disappearing into the green
Water; and the expensive delicate ship that must have seen
Something amazing, a boy falling out of the sky,
Had somewhere to get to and sailed calmly on.

Landscape with the Fall of Icarus

Williams uses seven short tercets, heavy enjambment, and no conventional punctuation. Icarus appears only at the end.

Landscape with the Fall of Icarus

William Carlos Williams

From Pictures from Brueghel and Other Poems (1962).

According to Brueghel
when Icarus fell
it was spring

a farmer was ploughing
his field
the whole pageantry

of the year was
awake tingling
near

the edge of the sea
concerned
with itself

sweating in the sun
that melted
the wings’ wax

unsignificantly
off the coast
there was

a splash quite unnoticed
this was
Icarus drowning
