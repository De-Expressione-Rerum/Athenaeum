Course

Units

36 micro-units, arranged within the macro units.

I. Classical Inheritance

Representation, public and private voice, argument, and memory.

  1. Unit 01 Ekphrasis I The Shield of Achilles
  2. Unit 02 Ekphrasis II The Shield of Aeneas
  3. Unit 03 Ekphrasis III Keats and Rilke
  4. Unit 04 Lyric and Desire Sappho
  5. Unit 05 Philosophical Poetry Lucretius, death, and argument
  6. Unit 06 Elegy and Memory Catullus and the language of mourning

II. Medieval Inheritance and Renaissance Lyric

Courtliness, romance, pilgrimage, devotion, allegory, and lyric form.

  1. Unit 07 Courtly Worlds Murasaki Shikibu and Bernart de Ventadorn
  2. Unit 08 Chivalric Romance Perceval, Gawain, and the quest
  3. Unit 09 Gothic Art and Pilgrimage Cathedrals, Dante, and Chaucer
  4. Unit 10 Devotional Grief The Stabat Mater and van der Weyden
  5. Unit 11 Allegory Everyman and The Faerie Queene
  6. Unit 12 The Renaissance Lyric Petrarch, Shakespeare, and the sonnet

III. Renaissance to Revolution

Order, religious fracture, theatricality, mortality, and political public art.

  1. Unit 13 Renaissance Art from Perspective to Mannerism Perspective, ideal form, and distortion
  2. Unit 14 Northern Renaissance and Reformation Bruegel, Holbein, humanism, iconoclasm
  3. Unit 15 Metaphysical Poetry and Baroque Art Donne, Marvell, sacred drama
  4. Unit 16 The Dutch Golden Age Observation, domestic life, and mortality
  5. Unit 17 Paradise Lost Milton and Protestant epic
  6. Unit 18 From Rococo to Revolution Neoclassicism, history painting, and political art

IV. Romanticism and Realism

Feeling, nature, the exceptional self, historical imagination, and social life.

  1. Unit 19 Sturm und Drang Goethe and the revolt of feeling
  2. Unit 20 The Sublime Burke, Coleridge, and terror
  3. Unit 21 Nature as Revelation Wordsworth, Blake, and visionary landscape
  4. Unit 22 The Romantic Hero Exile and the solitary self
  5. Unit 23 Victorian Medievalism and the Recovered Past Tennyson and the Pre-Raphaelites
  6. Unit 24 Revolution and Realism 1848, labor, and the elevation of the ordinary

V. Modern Life and Modern Seeing

Photography, empire, cities, and changing ways of seeing.

  1. Unit 25 Why Paint at All? Photography and the crisis of representation
  2. Unit 26 Art and Empire Orientalism, archaeology, exoticism, primitivism
  3. Unit 27 The Modern City Baudelaire and urban transformation
  4. Unit 28 Impressionism and Pointillism Light, perception, optics, impression
  5. Unit 29 Symbolism and the Fin de Siècle Mallarmé, Whistler, and suggestion
  6. Unit 30 After Impressionism Post-Impressionism and the move to Expressionism

VI. Avant-Garde and After

Cubism, machines, war, Dada, modernist fragmentation, and postwar abstraction.

  1. Unit 31 Cubism Multiple viewpoints
  2. Unit 32 Motion, Machine, and Image Futurism, Vorticism, Imagism
  3. Unit 33 World War I The shattered image
  4. Unit 34 Dada and Surrealism Anti-art, chance, and dream
  5. Unit 35 Modernist Poetry and Abstraction Fragmentation and abstraction
  6. Unit 36 Postwar Poetry and Abstraction Expression after modernism
